A self-taught linguist, child prodigy, practical scientist, a devoted teacher, turbulent academic, social reformer, a poet, a psychologist, humanitarian, religious  thinker and  a good family man, these are the characters which are found in 1 of 135 million male. 

Who is this mind blowing male with these characteristics? 

Well, surprise, it is an English mathematician who's very famous for the discovery of mathematical logic called "Boolean algebra" the one and only GEORGE BOOLE.

Born on November 2, 1815 at Lincoln, Lincolnshire, England. Boole was an English mathematician who created the logic that silently runs whenever we turn our phone on or use any technology. The logic is now called BOOLEAN ALGEBRA, which is the basis for the design of digital computer circuits. 

## Early Life

At the young age of 16 years he became his family's sole breadwinner after his father's business failed, teaching in village schools in the west riding of Yorkshire. Since, Boole was forced to leave school at the age of sixteen and never attended a university. He taught himself languages, natural philosophy and mathematics. He learned Latin, Greek, French, and German languages with which he unlocked classical text. At the age of 20, he established his own academy in Lincoln. He would be a schoolmaster for the next 15 years, until 1849 when he became a professor at the newly opened Queen’s University in Cork, Ireland. During scant leisure time, he read mathematics journals in Lincoln's Mechanics Institute. His reads included Isaac Newton, Pierre-Simon Laplace and Joseph-Louis Lagrange.

## Family

George came from a modest working class family but established an intellectual dynasty. He was the firstborn among four siblings born to John Boole Sr..(1777-1848) and Mary Ann Joyce(1780-1854). His father John was a local shoemaker with a deep passion for mathematics and science, who also happen to be the one to teach George his first math lessons.

In 1855, George Married Mary Everest Boole, another self-taught mathematician and educational theorist. They had 5 daughters who broke Victorian gender barriers to become prominent scientists, mathematicians and writers: Mary Ellen, Margaret, Alicia, Lucy and Ethel Lilian. 

His recent and most famous family member of descendent is Dr. Geoffrey Hinton, who's widely known as the "Godfather of AI". Hinton won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2024 for his pioneering breakthroughs in artificial neutral networks and deep learning 

## Contributions

Boole's greatest contribution was the **invention of symbolic logic and Boolean algebra**, which fundamentally altered how humans process data. His primary achievements include: 

- **The Algebra of Logic:** He treated logical arguments like mathematical equations, using variables to represent concepts.
- **Binary Concept:** He reduced logic to two values: 1 (True / Presence) and 0 (False / Absence).
- **Logical Operators:** He introduced the core functions AND, OR, and NOT.
- **Calculus and Probability:** He founded algebraic invariant theory and revolutionized the mathematical approach to differential equations and probabilities

In 1844, Boole’s paper ‘On a General Method in Analysis’ won the first gold prize for mathematics awarded by the Royal Society.

His own work on Differential Equations was used as a textbook along with Potts’ School Euclid, Wolstenholme’s Solid Geometry and the works of Isaac Todhunter.

While not heavily active in the College administration as an officer, he was a member of the library committee.

He also was involved in local life, being president of the Cork Cuvierian Society. 

He was awarded the Keith Medal by the Royal Society of Edinburgh in 1855 and was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1857. 

After his appointment to QCC, he received honorary degrees of LL.D. from the University of Dublin (Trinity College) in 1851 and that of D.C.L. from the University of Oxford.

## Discovery

Traditional logic, dating back to Aristotle, relied strictly on verbal philosophy. Boole noticed an exact mathematical analogy in how human thought processes statements. He realized that human reasoning could be systematically calculated if translated into algebraic symbols.

Boole pointed out the analog between algebraic symbols and those that can represent logical forms of syllogisms, showing how the symbols of quantity can be separated from those of operations. With Boole began the algebra of logic, or what is now called Boolean algebra. Boole's original and remarkable genera; symbolic method of logical inference, fully stated in Laws of Thought (1854), enables one, given any propositions involving any number of terms, to draw conclusions that are logically contained in the premises. His abstruse reasoning has led to applications of which he never dreamed like telephone switching and electronic computers use binary digits and Boolean logic for their design and operation.

Now, you must be wondering how a personality like him passes away prematurely at the age of 49. Simply put, he contracted pneumonia after walking three miles from his home to Queen's College in a rainstorm on November 24, 1864. He died on December 8, believing his algebraic logic was simply an abstract, beautiful philosophical exercise. He never lived to see its practical application.

However, in 1937, American engineer Claude Shannon realized Boole's 1 and 0 logic perfectly matched electronic routing switches. Today, every laptop, smartphone, and artificial intelligence model relies entirely on Boolean logic gates. As a self-taught shoemaker's son, Boole unknowingly drafted the foundational master code for the modern digital world.
